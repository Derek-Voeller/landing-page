Watch on your TV

Put the movies on the big screen and keep chat on your phone. Set this up before sign-on if you can; it takes a minute.

Direct stream link

The raw stream, for VLC, a TV browser, or anything that plays video links.

A iPhone or iPad → AirPlay

For an Apple TV or a TV with AirPlay built in.

  1. Make sure your phone and TV are on the same Wi-Fi.
  2. Open the Watch page in Safari and start the player.
  3. Tap the AirPlay icon in the player (a screen with a triangle) and pick your TV. Or open Control Center, tap Screen Mirroring, and pick your TV.
  4. Once the picture is on the TV, you can scroll down to chat on your phone.

No AirPlay icon? Open the direct stream link in Safari. It plays full screen with the AirPlay button in the controls. Go back to the Watch page tab to chat; the TV keeps playing.

B Android → Chromecast

For a Chromecast, Google TV, or a TV with Chromecast built in.

  1. Make sure your phone and TV are on the same Wi-Fi.
  2. Open the Watch page in Chrome and start the player.
  3. If the player shows a cast icon, tap it and pick your TV.
  4. No cast icon? Open the Google Home app, pick your TV, tap Cast my screen, then go back to Chrome and make the player full screen.

If that's choppy or won't connect: install the free VLC app, copy the direct stream link above, then in VLC choose More → New stream, paste it, and tap the cast icon to send it to your TV. Chat stays on the Watch page in Chrome.

C Smart TV or computer

For a TV with a web browser, or a laptop plugged into the TV.

  1. Open the TV's or computer's web browser and go to .
  2. Press play, then use the full-screen button.

Or use VLC on a computer: Media → Open Network Stream (Windows) or File → Open Network (Mac), and paste the direct stream link.

If something's off

  • A few seconds behind chat? Normal. Everyone sees the stream 10–30 seconds behind real time.
  • "Please stand by" on screen? We're fixing it. The Watch page brings the picture back by itself.
  • TV stopped after a stand-by? Cast or AirPlay again; the TV doesn't always reconnect on its own.
